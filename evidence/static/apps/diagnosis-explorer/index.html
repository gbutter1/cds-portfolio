About this app

What it shows. Visits from six fictional Atlanta-area facilities (an emergency department, an urgent care, a family practice, a pediatric practice, an internal medicine group and a community health center) across two flu seasons, October 2024 to September 2026. Each facility has its own patient mix, so the top diagnoses differ the way they would in real life: ear infections and checkups at pediatrics, chest and abdominal pain in the ER, high blood pressure and diabetes in internal medicine.

What's real. The diagnosis codes and descriptions are the official ICD-10-CM code set, and the Georgia flu line is real surveillance data from the CDC's ILINet network, refreshed every time the pipeline runs. The individual visits are realistic sample data, so no patient information is involved.

How the alert works. For each facility, the alert baseline is its normal off-season flu share (June to September) plus two standard deviations, with a minimum of 1% of visits. Any week above that line is flagged. This mirrors how the CDC defines a flu season's start and end.
